Then write the summary inside <summary> tags, with these sections:

1. Request and intent: everything asked of you, in detail, with the standing instructions and constraints that still hold.
2. Decisions: what was decided and why. Where an approach was rejected or corrected, say what replaced it.
3. Facts and references: the names, paths, identifiers, commands, values and exact strings established so far. Quote them exactly instead of describing them.
4. Work done: what was done, in order, what came of it, and what was changed and where.
5. Problems and fixes: the errors met, how each was resolved, and what did not work.
6. Prompts received: every prompt sent to you that was not a tool result, quoted or closely paraphrased, in order.
7. Remaining work: what was asked for and is not done yet.
8. Current state: exactly what was being worked on just before this request, and the next step where one follows directly from the latest prompt.

Write the summary in the language of the conversation. Its length follows the conversation's: say everything a later turn would need, exactly, and nothing the conversation does not hold. Keep the analysis as short as the conversation allows, and leave out a section that has nothing to say.
